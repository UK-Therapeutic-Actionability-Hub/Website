Assessments

The UK Therapeutic Actionability Hub provides a structured process for assessing potential therapeutic opportunities for individuals living with rare genetic conditions.

Who can submit a case?

Cases can be submitted for consideration where there is a confirmed genetic diagnosis.

Cases can be submitted by patients, family members, patient organisations, researchers, and clinicians. While we are focused on UK individuals for therapy development, we will assess non-UK cases.

How to submit a case

Cases are submitted through our secure online submission form. The form collects the information required by the Hub to undertake an initial assessment of the case.

How we assess cases

Each submission is reviewed to evaluate the potential therapeutic actionability of the genetic finding. At the initial step, we solely evaluate the gentic variant and the gene-disease relationship. Should further clinical evaluation be warranted, we will reach out to the submitter and engage our national multidisciplinary treatment board.

Each case will be assessed by a dedicated team of trained assessors and carefully be evaluated across different therapeutic modalities. We will further check whether any therapies are already available, availability of clinical trials and research programmes globally.

What happens after an assessment?

Following assessment, we will provide feedback summarising the outcome of the review and, where appropriate, potential next steps.

An assessment does not necessarily mean that a therapeutic option will be identified. In some cases, the available evidence may not currently support a therapeutic approach. Where a potentially actionable avenue is identified, further investigation or discussion with relevant experts may be recommended.

Turnaround times

We aim to complete the initial assessments within 2 - 4 weeks of receiving the information required to review a case.

Some assessments may take longer where additional information or specialist expertise is required. We will contact the submitter if further information is needed during the assessment.

Important information

The Hub's assessment is intended to support evaluation of potential therapeutic opportunities. It does not replace clinical assessment, medical advice or decision-making by the clinical team responsible for an individual's care.
